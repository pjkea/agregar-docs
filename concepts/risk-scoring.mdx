## Score architecture

Every transaction receives a **composite risk score** from 0 to 300, built from three independent layers that are summed and capped:

```
composite = min(customer_score + transaction_score + behavioral_score, 300)
```

| Layer | Max contribution | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Customer risk** | 100 | Static profile: customer type, occupation, jurisdiction, PEP status, KYC quality |
| **Transaction risk** | 100 | Per-event signals: amount, channel, destination, type, timing |
| **Behavioral risk** | 100 | The higher of: anomaly-detection rule contributions, or the ML model score |

---

## Decision thresholds

The composite score maps to a decision:

| Score | Decision | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 0–89 | `ALLOW` | Transaction proceeds normally |
| 90–119 | `FLAG` | Alert created; transaction proceeds; analyst queued for review |
| 120–149 | `STEP_UP` | Step-up verification requested before transaction is released |
| 150–199 | `HOLD` | Transaction held pending manual review |
| 200–300 | `BLOCK` | Transaction blocked |

<Note>
  Thresholds are configurable per jurisdiction. The values above are the defaults for Ghana (GHA).
</Note>

---

## Override triggers

Certain conditions **tighten the decision thresholds** (making a `HOLD` or `BLOCK` far more likely) and can trigger their own workflows, regardless of the base score. Sanctions, PEP, and adverse-media signals come from the [Screening API](/compliance/sanctions), not a fixed score bump:

| Trigger | Effect |
|---|---|
| Sanctions hit (Screening API) | Strictest thresholds applied → typically `BLOCK`; a SANCTIONS case is opened automatically |
| PEP hit (Screening API) | Stricter (PEP) thresholds applied; an AML case is opened automatically |
| Adverse-media hit (Screening API) | Stricter thresholds applied |
| High-value cash (above CTR threshold) | Stricter thresholds; a CTR is drafted automatically |
| FATF / high-risk counterparty jurisdiction | Adds transaction-risk points |
| Travel Rule threshold exceeded | Travel Rule originator/beneficiary fields required |

## Customer risk layer

Static, slow-changing customer attributes (0–100). Five components are summed and capped at 100:

| Component | Range | How it scores |
|---|---|---|
| Customer type | 0–25 | Individual 10 · Company 15 · Merchant 25 |
| Occupation | 0–25 | Low-risk 5 · unknown 10 · medium-risk 15 · high-risk 25 |
| Geography (home country) | 0–20 | Low-risk 0 · unknown 5 · medium-risk 10 · high-risk 20 |
| PEP status | 0–20 | PEP 20 · non-PEP 0 |
| KYC quality | 0–25 | Good (71–100) 0 · medium (41–70) 10 · poor (0–40) 20 · plus 5 if KYC is missing or older than 2 years |

<Note>
  KYC quality is the single biggest lever on a customer's static risk — poor or stale KYC starts a customer 20–25 points higher. Supply `customer_kyc_score` at ingestion so this scores accurately.
</Note>

---

## Transaction risk layer

Per-event signals from the transaction itself (0–100). Five components are summed and capped at 100:

| Component | Range | How it scores |
|---|---|---|
| Amount | 0–30 | Banded against jurisdiction thresholds (low 5 → critical 30) |
| Channel | 0–25 | Bank 5 · Card 10 · ATM 15 · Momo 20 · Agent 25 |
| Destination country | 0–20 | Low-risk 0 · unknown 5 · medium-risk 10 · high-risk 20 |
| Transaction type | 0–15 | Weighted by type |
| Timing | 0–12 | Off-hours / unusual timing add points |

---

## Behavioral risk layer

Unlike the two layers above, behavioral risk is **not a fixed point table**. It is the higher of two independent signals (0–100):

```
behavioral = max(rule_contributions, ml_score)
```

- **Rule contributions** — the anomaly-detection rules that fire on this transaction (velocity and burst patterns, structuring, device sharing, counterparty anomalies, deviation from the customer's 30-day baseline, …) each add a contribution; the total is capped at 100.
- **ML score** — the ensemble model (XGBoost / CatBoost / LightGBM / River, scaled 0–100) catches patterns the rules miss.

Taking the max means a strong rule signal **or** a strong model signal is enough on its own. The individual rules are covered in [Detection Rules](/concepts/detection-rules).

## Score in the response

```json
{
  "transaction_id": "TXN-A1B2C3",
  "combined_risk_score": 127,
  "decision": "STEP_UP",
  "customer_risk_score": 45,
  "transaction_risk_score": 55,
  "behavioral_risk_score": 27,
  "risk_factors": [
    "PEP account",
    "Amount 3.2σ above 30-day mean",
    "Cross-border to NG"
  ],
  "ml_score": 0.74,
  "model_used": "ensemble"
}
```
